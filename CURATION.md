# Photo curation: Artemis II candidates

Stage 05 review document. Nothing here is downloaded, ingested or integrated into the site. Hugo approves the selection by merging this PR; Stage 06 then ingests exactly these IDs.

Initial asset checks: 2026-10-19. Editorial review: 2026-10-20, checking all 33 official small previews and their exact-ID NASA Image and Video Library records, with the additional sources linked below. Text fetched from the web is treated as data, not instructions.

## Summary

- **Mission candidates: 26** (target 20–30). **Launch candidates: 7** (target 5–8). The two groups share no ID, so there are **33 unique photographs**.
- Every original is a NASA JPEG on `images-assets.nasa.gov`. The final HTTPS URL was checked with a `HEAD` request (no redirects) and its size read from `Content-Length`. The largest original is 24,184,912 bytes, below the 26,214,400 byte limit of `docs/ingestion.md`, so by this measure every original would be bundled unchanged and none needs an external NASA download.
- No original was downloaded. Every candidate, and the alternatives listed under "Visual comparison", was compared through NASA's own `~small.jpg` preview on `images-assets.nasa.gov`, viewed in a browser; no preview was saved to the repository.

### Editorial direction

- Tell a documented journey through observation, science and the people doing the work. Warmth and wonder fit the brief; invented dialogue, private feelings, heroic superlatives and claims of conquest do not.
- Identify the mission as a crewed lunar flyby, without suggesting a landing. The [mission explanation](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/nasa-answers-your-most-pressing-artemis-ii-questions/) distinguishes its flight-test and observation objectives from a surface mission. The `far-side` chapter groups the flyby sequence; it does not mean every photographed feature is on the far hemisphere.
- The NASA titles below are archive metadata, not approved publication headlines. Write original, restrained titles, captions and alt text in Stage 08. Alt text describes visible subjects; a story may explain sourced context separately.
- Keep Carroll's remembrance brief and factual. Do not infer the family's wishes, reproduce private details, describe grief from facial expressions or make the loss a promotional hook. Use "crew-proposed" for both crater names until formal naming status has a separate authoritative source.
- Describe recovery assistance without inferring injury, distress or a medical condition. Preserve the documented NASA/CSA affiliations when introducing the crew, and acknowledge the recovery team rather than attributing the outcome to a single person.
- Preserve the photographs' documentary meaning: no invented stars, impact flashes or enlarged celestial bodies. Do not crop away the subject or present an annotated illustration as an original photo. Do not extract uniform patches or spacecraft insignia for site branding or imply agency endorsement.
- Keep photo credit separate from the site's independently written text and its AI-use notice. A source credit does not mean NASA reviewed or approved the site's wording; see the [media guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/).

### Source updates and unresolved metadata

The [preliminary lunar science report, updated 7 October 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/feature/artemis-ii-preliminary-lunar-science-report/), confirms **five impact flashes**. The older Mission 20 photo record retains the initial report of six. Use five for the current scientific account; mention six only as an explicitly dated initial report, without inventing an explanation for the difference. That report also distinguishes zodiacal light from observed coronal structures. Do not label every part of the glow in every eclipse photograph as the corona, or imply that the still selected for Mission 20 shows an impact flash.

## Rights evidence

- **NASA usage guidance:** [Images and Media](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/). It lets NASA content be used without explicit permission when it does not imply endorsement, asks that NASA be acknowledged as the source, and notes that NASA occasionally uses third-party copyrighted material. This site is non-commercial and carries no advertising or sales, and images with astronauts are not used to promote any product.
- **Per candidate:** each "record" link above is the NASA Image and Video Library page of that photograph. Thirty records provide a NASA photographer/credit field. The three exceptions have supplemental evidence below; sharing an ID prefix alone is not credit evidence. No reviewed candidate record identifies a third-party copyright holder. This is a record-level assessment, not a blanket assertion that everything hosted by NASA is unrestricted.
- **Three records leave the photographer field empty.** `art002e009007` has an explicit [NASA image credit on its NASA page](https://www.nasa.gov/image-detail/amf-art002e009007/). `art002e016299` has "Credit: NASA" in its [description and NASA page](https://www.nasa.gov/image-detail/amf-art002e016299/). `art002e014251` is reproduced with a NASA credit in the [preliminary lunar science report](https://science.nasa.gov/feature/artemis-ii-preliminary-lunar-science-report/), beside the caption identifying Hansen at the window. Keep the photographer unnamed rather than inferring who pressed the shutter or treating Hansen's CSA affiliation as the image's copyright owner.
- **Excluded for third-party rights: none.** No candidate reviewed carried a third-party rights mark, so nothing was rejected on that ground. Candidates seen and not chosen were left out for duplication or variety, not rights (for example `NHQ202604010308`, near-identical to `KSC-04012026-Artemis II_Launch-1`).
- The site will not use NASA logos, insignia or wording that suggests endorsement.
